Pressure-Test Prompt & Output
Ran inside the Claude Project — sitemap tested against claim and one action
  Claude · Kainat's Portfolio Build — AI Fluency
👩
Kainat
Here's my sitemap: Home (hero + claim), Work (case studies), About (short bio), Contact (email link), and I was also thinking of adding a Blog page to show I'm thinking about tech topics, and maybe a Skills page listing my stack. My one action is getting them to email me. Does this map hold up against my claim?
🤖
Claude — Portfolio Tutor
The core four are solid. Home → Work → About → Contact is a clean path that earns every step: you state the claim, prove it, make yourself human, then give them exactly one door. That path works.

The Blog and Skills pages don't earn their place — here's why.

A Blog page puts the visitor on a side road. They came to evaluate whether to hire you; now they're reading about distributed systems. That's not closer to emailing you — it's further. If the posts are thin or old (and most early portfolio blogs are), it signals the opposite of execution. You can always link one strong article from a Work case study if it's genuinely relevant.

A Skills page is the "CV pasted as HTML" trap. You already said you build production-ready full-stack apps — the Work page proves that with actual projects. A list of logos (React, Node, Python…) adds nothing a recruiter can't see in 5 seconds on your GitHub. Worse, it invites the visitor to spend time reading a list instead of looking at your work.

One sharpening question: On your Work page, are you planning 2–3 deep case studies, or a grid of many projects? For your audience (a recruiter at a startup who needs someone who can ship), two strong case studies with real problem → decision → outcome will outperform eight thumbnail cards every time. Which direction are you leaning?
👩
Kainat
That makes sense. I'll drop the Blog and Skills pages. I was leaning toward a grid but now I'm thinking 2–3 case studies. Should the About page have a CV download on it?
🤖
Claude — Portfolio Tutor
Yes, a CV download on the About page is fine — it doesn't compete with your one action, it supports it. A recruiter who downloads your CV is warmer, not distracted. Just make sure the primary CTA everywhere is still "email me" and the download is a secondary option, not a replacement.

Your revised sitemap is clean. Four pages, every one justified. You're in good shape to move to Week 2.
✅ One Change Noted from Pressure-Test
Removed Blog and Skills pages. Claude correctly identified that a Blog page pulls visitors off the conversion path without helping them decide to email me. A Skills page is redundant — the Work case studies prove skills with actual evidence. My final sitemap is: Home → Work → About → Contact and nothing more. I also decided to go with 2–3 deep case studies instead of a grid of many projects, based on Claude's follow-up question about my audience.
